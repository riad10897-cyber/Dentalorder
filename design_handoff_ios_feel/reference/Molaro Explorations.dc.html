NCurrent app vs. a full marketplace — pros and cons

What carries over

  • Money handling is already built properly. Totals are worked out on the server, prices are locked at order time, and an order is refused if prices change while a student is checking out. A marketplace needs all of this, and most of it is already done.
  • Data security. Every table has access rules set at the database level (row-level security), and there are scripts that check them. Students can't see each other's orders, and sellers can't see other sellers' orders.
  • The paper look stands out and is easy to read. Body text is Atkinson Hyperlegible, sizes are in rem so they follow the phone's text-size setting, and there are settings for reduced motion and high contrast. It's unlike other dental-supply apps.
  • Parts that work for any product. The bottom sheet with real swipe physics, the settings menu, consistent screen headers, empty states, undo, and the price-change notices.
  • One seller per order keeps pickup and payment simple. A per-seller cart is a natural first step for a marketplace.

What holds it back

  • The tooth chart is the order screen. Products are "extras" listed under the chart, so a student who only wants burs still lands on a chart of teeth.
  • Home is a list of sellers, not products. There's no search, no category browsing and no comparing one product across sellers. Those are the core marketplace actions, and the current rules rule them out.
  • The accent colours are all spoken for. Red means Frasaco, blue means Copy A and green means handed over. Nothing is left for sale prices, stock, categories or brands.
  • No product images. Thirty plain-text rows of gloves are hard to scan, and suppliers will expect photos.
  • The weekly order is built into the data. An order with extras is still saved as order_type = 'tooth', and each order carries a week number. An order with only products has no proper place of its own.
  • Five fixed categories, and only an admin can change them. That's fine for 10 products, not for 300.
  • No commission. That's deliberate for now, but it means the screens have no place for fees, which a marketplace may eventually need.

A way to bridge it

Design suggestions only, nothing built.

Make the seller's storefront the main unit. Each seller gets a shop page. The tooth chart becomes one product in it: "Typodont teeth", which opens the chart for picking. Burs and gloves sit next to it with equal weight.

Home becomes Shop: search, categories, and "Reorder your teeth" pinned at the top for the weekly habit, so the main use case stays one tap away.

Rename order_type 'tooth' to something like 'seller_order' and make the week number optional before adding product-only orders. Keep the colour rule, but add a neutral system for categories so the tooth colours stay reserved for teeth.
